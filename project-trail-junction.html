Trail Junction

Plan before you lose service. Make sense of unfamiliar junctions. Remember what you found along the way.

#BuildHybridAI 2026 · React / TypeScript / Firebase AI Logic / Gemini / PWA

Try Trail Junction ↗View on GitHub ↗

Plan for the trail

Search U.S. trails, preview their routes, and download maps before heading out. Explore recommends trails based on saved maps and logged hikes. A cached production build keeps downloaded maps, manual sign entry, and the local journal available offline.

Read the junction

Photograph a trail sign, review its destinations and arrows, and highlight a matching place or branch on a saved map. The Rampart Ridge demo uses a real National Park Service sign to walk through this flow, connecting sign interpretation with map context.

Keep the memories

The photo journal brings hike notes, photos, and captions together. Optional accounts support cross-device sync through Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore. Cloud Gemini can suggest identifications and short nature notes for photos of plants, fungi, and wildlife.

Hybrid AI with a manual fallback

Firebase AI Logic uses Gemini Nano to read signs on compatible desktop Chrome after the model is downloaded. When local inference is unavailable and the device is online, it falls back to cloud Gemini. On phones and tablets, AI sign reading requires internet access; downloaded maps and manual sign entry still work offline.

  1. Save before leaving service. Trail geometry comes from USGS, with USGS Topo or NPS maps for downloads. A service worker caches the app alongside the offline workflow.
  2. Interpret and review. The app extracts sign destinations and arrows, then lets the hiker review the result and select a matching map place or branch.
  3. Local journal, optional sync. Notes and photos remain useful on the device, while signed-in users can sync journal content across devices.
